Now the soda. Three fifty. Refills included, if you're lucky.

You probably think the burger is where the restaurant makes its money. It's the expensive part, right? Meat, bun, a cook, a grill.

In a typical small sit-down restaurant, food and non-alcoholic drinks eat up about a third of everything the place takes in. That's the median in the National Restaurant Association's own data.

The soda is a different story. In the trade press, one beverage executive gave the math. A fountain soda that sells for two fifty makes about two fifteen in gross profit. [PAUSE]

That's eighty-six cents of gross profit on every dollar. Syrup, water, ice, a cup. You paid three fifty for it.

So the burger is the bait. The soda is the business.

And to be fair, the restaurant isn't getting rich either. A full-service restaurant usually keeps somewhere between one and five cents of every dollar as actual profit. Rent, staff, insurance, the grill that breaks every summer.

But notice something. So far, you agreed to every dollar. You said yes to the bacon. You said yes to the soda.

The next dollars are the ones you never said yes to.
