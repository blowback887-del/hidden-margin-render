The server turns the screen toward you. Twenty percent. Twenty-two. Twenty-five. You tap twenty. Five dollars and eighteen cents.

But twenty percent of what? Not your food. This screen calculated the tip on the whole total. The tax. The kitchen fee. You just tipped on a fee.

It's a setting. Toast's own help pages say the restaurant chooses whether suggested tips are calculated before or after tax. In March, Fox News reported that Dave and Buster's screens were suggesting tips on the after-tax total. Another restaurant owner, asked about it, called that double dipping.

And it works. In a study of New York taxis, screens with higher suggested buttons, twenty, twenty-five, thirty instead of fifteen, twenty, twenty-five, pushed tips up by roughly ten percent.

So who makes these machines? In a huge number of restaurants, it's one company. The same Toast with the upselling leaderboard. About one hundred eighty-nine thousand locations run on its system.

And last month, the Federal Trade Commission and eight states took Toast to court. [PAUSE]

According to the complaint, for almost two years, Toast added its own ninety-nine cent fee to online orders over ten dollars. The diners weren't properly told. Neither were the restaurants. And the money went to Toast. Not the restaurant. Back then, Fox Business reported it showed up even on orders through a restaurant's own website.

The complaint says the fee ran from 2021 to the summer of 2023. Toast strongly disagrees with the lawsuit, and a court will decide.

Back in 2023, a restaurant owner in Missouri told Fox Business he'd lost seventy percent of his online orders. "It appears to the customer like we're penalizing them for ordering online," he said.

Remember those three to five cents the restaurant keeps from every dollar? For its last quarter alone, Toast reported one hundred sixty-nine million dollars in profit. [PAUSE]
