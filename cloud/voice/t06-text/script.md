# T06 — The Menu Said $14. The Bill Said $31. — сценарий (версия 2, после фактчекера)
Окраска: сатира (абсурдные сборы, корпоративный язык) + детектив как приправа («найдём каждый из семнадцати долларов»). Ресторан не злодей — схема.
Сквозная линия: один заказ, путь бургера от меню до терминала; каждый шаг — новый доллар в чеке. Предмет-талисман: чек, который растёт снизу по строке.
Строки `> SEE:` — что видим (не озвучиваются).

## PART 1

The menu said fourteen. The card machine took thirty-one dollars and eight cents. [PAUSE]
> SEE: меню, строка «Classic Burger 14»; резкий переход — терминал, на экране $31.08

Here's a typical order, built from real fees. One burger, one soda, a couple of add-ons. No dessert, no cocktail. And somewhere between the menu and the machine, seventeen dollars showed up. You've had this happen. You just never traced it.
> SEE: чек начинает печататься; сверху «$14», снизу пустые строки; мелкая плашка «Illustrative example · real fees»

The first one was already waiting before you sat down. Look at that menu again. Fourteen. Not fourteen dollars. Just *fourteen*.
> SEE: крупно цена «14» без знака доллара; знак $ рядом зачёркивается

Cornell researchers tested exactly this in one restaurant. Same food, three versions of the menu. The people who got prices without a dollar sign spent about eight percent more. [PAUSE]
> SEE: скриншот новости Cornell; три меню рядом: «$20», «20», «twenty dollars»; над «20» — «+8%»

So let's walk your order from the menu to the machine, and find every single one of those seventeen dollars.

## PART 2

That missing dollar sign has a name in the business. Menu engineering. There are consultants who do this for a living, and one well-known menu engineer, Gregg Rapp, tells clients to drop the dollar signs, the decimal points and the cents.
> SEE: слово «MENU ENGINEERING» штампом; цитата-плашка Rapp «omit dollar signs, decimal points, and cents» с источником

The researchers think the reason is simple. A dollar sign reminds you that you're paying. Take it away, and fourteen is just a number. A nice, friendly number.
> SEE: знак $ превращается в монету и падает; «14» улыбается

Now look at the top of the page. A tomahawk steak. Fifty-eight. Almost nobody orders the fifty-eight dollar steak. That's the point.
> SEE: меню, наверху «Tomahawk Ribeye 58» в рамке; камера скользит вниз к бургеру

Writer William Poundstone calls it an anchor. A ridiculously expensive item they don't expect anyone to buy. Its job is to sit there and make everything below it look like a bargain. Next to fifty-eight, fourteen feels like a steal.
> SEE: весы: 58 тянет вниз, 14 подпрыгивает вверх с подписью «bargain?»

Notice the prices aren't lined up in a neat column, either. Poundstone says that's on purpose. Give people a column of prices, and the price-conscious ones run straight down it and pick from the three cheapest things.
> SEE: две версии меню: цены колонкой справа (палец ползёт вниз к трём дешёвым) против цен, спрятанных в описаниях

Then the server comes over. Great choice. Want to add bacon? Three dollars. Truffle fries instead of regular? Two more.
> SEE: чек: «+ bacon 3.00», «+ truffle fries 2.00»

That question is not small talk. Toast, a company whose system runs a huge number of restaurants, publishes training advice for restaurants. It says the register itself can remind staff to ask those upselling questions. It even suggests a leaderboard for the server who sells the most add-ons. [PAUSE]
> SEE: скриншот блога Toast, выделены строки про reminding employees и leaderboard

There's a leaderboard. For your bacon.
> SEE: таблица лидеров с именами-заглушками, «Add-ons sold»; полоска бекона едет вверх

And here's the principle behind this whole video. A menu price isn't the price. It's the opening bid. Like an airline's base fare. The real number gets built after you've already said yes.
> SEE: плашка «MENU PRICE = OPENING BID»; рядом билет на самолёт «base fare $49»

## PART 3

Now the soda. Three fifty. Refills included, if you're lucky.
> SEE: стакан газировки, ценник 3.50; чек: «Soda 3.50», подытог 22.50

You probably think the burger is where the restaurant makes its money. It's the expensive part, right? Meat, bun, a cook, a grill.
> SEE: бургер в разрезе, над слоями ценники

In a typical small sit-down restaurant, food and non-alcoholic drinks eat up about a third of everything the place takes in. That's the median in the National Restaurant Association's own data.
> SEE: круговая диаграмма «33.7% food & beverage cost»; источник NRA

The soda is a different story. In the trade press, one beverage executive gave the math. A fountain soda that sells for two fifty makes about two fifteen in gross profit. [PAUSE]
> SEE: скриншот статьи Modern Restaurant Management; «$2.50 → $2.15 profit»

That's eighty-six cents of gross profit on every dollar. Syrup, water, ice, a cup. You paid three fifty for it.
> SEE: стакан разбирается на части: сироп, вода, лёд, стакан; итог «≈ 86¢ of every $1»

So the burger is the bait. The soda is the business.
> SEE: бургер на крючке; газировка в сейфе

And to be fair, the restaurant isn't getting rich either. A full-service restaurant usually keeps somewhere between one and five cents of every dollar as actual profit. Rent, staff, insurance, the grill that breaks every summer.
> SEE: доллар режется на части; остаются 1–5 центов; подписи rent, staff, insurance

But notice something. So far, you agreed to every dollar. You said yes to the bacon. You said yes to the soda.
> SEE: чек: каждая строка с галочкой «you said yes»

The next dollars are the ones you never said yes to.
> SEE: чек допечатывает новую строку, она пока размыта

## PART 4

Down at the bottom of the bill, under the food, there's a line that says "Kitchen Appreciation Fee." Three and a half percent. Seventy-nine cents.
> SEE: строка чека «Kitchen Appreciation Fee 3.5% — 0.79» подсвечивается

That's not on the menu, unless you count the tiny line at the very bottom. Consumer advocate Christopher Elliott has collected names for these. A health mandate fee. A wellness surcharge. And the kitchen appreciation fee.
> SEE: скриншот статьи Elliott; названия сборов выпадают на экран как наклейки

My favorite came from one of his readers. At a steakhouse in Moab, Utah, he found a "beef-cost surcharge" on his bill. He'd ordered chicken. [PAUSE]
> SEE: курица на тарелке; рядом наклейка «Beef-Cost Surcharge»

The restaurant took it off when he objected. Many people never notice it at all.
> SEE: чек кладут на стол лицевой стороной вниз

Sometimes a state does see it. In 2023, Arizona's attorney general went after two Scottsdale restaurants over a three and a half percent "employee benefits charge" that wasn't on the menu. They paid twenty thousand dollars and had to start printing it.
> SEE: пресс-релиз генпрокурора Аризоны (azag.gov); «3.5% — $20,000»

So isn't this illegal? Depends on where you're sitting. California passed a law banning hidden fees. Then, two days before it took effect, restaurants got an exception. They can keep the fee, as long as it's written on the menu.
> SEE: карта США; Калифорния: «SB 478 — July 1, 2024», поверх штамп «EXCEPT RESTAURANTS»

Florida's new law, from July of this year, doesn't ban these fees either. It just says show them before people order. Only a handful of states, like Massachusetts, Minnesota and now Colorado, require mandatory fees to be in the menu price.
> SEE: карта: Флорида «disclose»; Массачусетс, Миннесота, Колорадо светятся зелёным «fees in the price»

And the federal junk fee rule? It covers concert tickets and hotels. Not restaurants.
> SEE: плашка FTC rule; «tickets ✓ hotels ✓ restaurants ✗»

Now, the tax. Eight percent, depending on where you live. In some places, it's charged on the fee too. One of Elliott's readers found a place in Hawaii that added fees, taxed the fees, and then put a card fee on top of all of it. Then comes a three percent credit card surcharge. Seventy-five cents.
> SEE: чек: «Tax 8% — 1.86», «Card surcharge 3% — 0.75», итог 25.90

Here's what the sign by the door won't tell you. Visa caps that surcharge at three percent. Connecticut, Maine and Massachusetts ban it outright. And it's only allowed on credit cards. Pay with debit, and that line isn't allowed. [PAUSE]
> SEE: кредитка с ценником 3%; дебетовая карта рядом, на ней «0%»

If this is the kind of thing you want to catch before it catches you, subscribe. We do one of these every few days.
> SEE: кнопка подписки, логотип канала

We're at twenty-five ninety. And the most expensive part of the bill hasn't even happened yet.
> SEE: чек «25.90»; терминал разворачивается к зрителю

## PART 5

The server turns the screen toward you. Twenty percent. Twenty-two. Twenty-five. You tap twenty. Five dollars and eighteen cents.
> SEE: терминал с кнопками 20% / 22% / 25%; палец жмёт 20%

But twenty percent of what? Not your food. This screen calculated the tip on the whole total. The tax. The kitchen fee. You just tipped on a fee.
> SEE: стрелки от строк налога и сбора сходятся в кнопку «20%»

It's a setting. Toast's own help pages say the restaurant chooses whether suggested tips are calculated before or after tax. In March, Fox News reported that Dave and Buster's screens were suggesting tips on the after-tax total. Another restaurant owner, asked about it, called that double dipping.
> SEE: скриншот справки Toast с переключателем; скриншот Fox News про Dave & Buster's

And it works. In a study of New York taxis, screens with higher suggested buttons, twenty, twenty-five, thirty instead of fifteen, twenty, twenty-five, pushed tips up by roughly ten percent.
> SEE: две панели такси: старые и новые кнопки; «+≈10%»

So who makes these machines? In a huge number of restaurants, it's one company. The same Toast with the upselling leaderboard. About one hundred eighty-nine thousand locations run on its system.
> SEE: логотип Toast нейтрально; счётчик «≈189,000 locations»

And last month, the Federal Trade Commission and eight states took Toast to court. [PAUSE]
> SEE: скриншот пресс-релиза FTC, 29 октября 2026

According to the complaint, for almost two years, Toast added its own ninety-nine cent fee to online orders over ten dollars. The diners weren't properly told. Neither were the restaurants. And the money went to Toast. Not the restaurant. Back then, Fox Business reported it showed up even on orders through a restaurant's own website.
> SEE: онлайн-заказ $10.50; внизу строка «+$0.99» уходит стрелкой к Toast, мимо ресторана

The complaint says the fee ran from 2021 to the summer of 2023. Toast strongly disagrees with the lawsuit, and a court will decide.
> SEE: плашка «Allegations — Toast disputes them»

Back in 2023, a restaurant owner in Missouri told Fox Business he'd lost seventy percent of his online orders. "It appears to the customer like we're penalizing them for ordering online," he said.
> SEE: витрина небольшой закусочной (сток); цитата-плашка с источником Fox Business

Remember those few cents the restaurant keeps from every dollar? For its last quarter alone, Toast reported one hundred sixty-nine million dollars in profit. [PAUSE]
> SEE: монетка 1–5¢ слева; справа столбик «$169M — Q3 2026»

## PART 6

So here's your receipt. Burger, fourteen. Everything else, seventeen dollars and eight cents. More than the burger itself.

Most of it you agreed to without noticing. Some of it nobody asked you about at all.
