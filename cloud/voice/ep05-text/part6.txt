In one LendingTree survey, about one in five holiday borrowers paid just the minimum. But the system is built so that she can. And whoever lends her that time gets paid for it. Which brings us to a small town in Pennsylvania, back in nineteen-oh-nine. A bank treasurer named Merkel Landis had an idea. He called it the Christmas Club. Working people brought a little money to the bank every week, all year long. Then, just before Christmas, they got it back. In time to buy presents. Back then, families paid for Christmas before it came. The bank held their savings. Today, we pay for Christmas after it's gone. The bank holds our future.
