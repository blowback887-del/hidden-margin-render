Hold that thought. Jenna is going to have a lovely Christmas. We'll come back for her. Before credit cards, before Black Friday, before Amazon, there was a problem with the calendar. In nineteen thirty-nine, November had five Thursdays. Thanksgiving would land on the thirtieth. Back then, stores didn't push Christmas shopping before Thanksgiving. So the shopping season would be short. And the country was still crawling out of the Great Depression. So the retailers did what retailers do. They lobbied the President. Franklin Roosevelt reportedly thought the idea sounded "silly." Then he did it anyway. He proclaimed Thanksgiving a week early. A national holiday. Moved. For shopping. People were furious. Critics called it "Franksgiving." The next year, thirty-two states followed the President and sixteen kept the old date. For a while, America couldn't even agree on when to eat the turkey. Eventually, Congress fixed it to the fourth Thursday. The shopping season got its head start, and it kept it. That same year, in Chicago, a department store had a different idea. Montgomery Ward asked one of its copywriters, a thirty-four-year-old named Robert May, to write a Christmas story the store could give away to shoppers. A freebie. A marketing gimmick. May wrote about a reindeer the others laughed at because of his shiny red nose. The store handed out two point four million copies in the first year. His name was Rudolph.
