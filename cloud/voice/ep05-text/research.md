## 1909 — Christmas Club
F7. December 1909: Merkel Landis, treasurer of Carlisle Trust Company (Carlisle, Pennsylvania), created the Christmas Savings Club after shoe-factory workers asked about a joint savings account. ~350 members, average ~$28 saved. Depositors paid in weekly through the year and got the money before Christmas.
   - https://njdnp.libraries.rutgers.edu/2018/12/21/tis-the-season-to-be-saving-christmas-savings-clubs-in-nj-newspapers
   - https://blogs.loc.gov/inside_adams/2019/12/saving-for-the-holidays-the-old-fashioned-way/
   - https://www.americanbanker.com/creditunions/news/christmas-clubs

## 1939 — Thanksgiving moved, Rudolph invented
F8. In 1939 November had five Thursdays; Thanksgiving would fall on Nov 30. Retail lobbyists (Lew Hahn, Retail Dry Goods Association) feared a short shopping season. In August 1939 FDR moved Thanksgiving a week earlier, to Nov 23. Critics called it "Franksgiving"; in 1940, 32 states followed the president and 16 kept the old date. Congress fixed the fourth Thursday in late 1941.
   - https://blog.newspapers.com/fdr-moves-thanksgiving/
   - https://historynet.com/fdrs-franksgiving-debacle/
   - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franksgiving
F9. AP (1939): FDR said the idea sounded "silly" but deferred to the merchants.
   - https://jewishworldreview.com/1121/fdr_thanksgiving.php3 (Washington Post / AP)
F10. 1939: Montgomery Ward asked copywriter Robert L. May, 34, to write a Christmas story to give away to shoppers as a promotional gimmick. 2.4 million copies distributed in the first year. Song by his brother-in-law Johnny Marks, recorded by Gene Autry in 1949.
   - https://newsdesk.si.edu/snapshot/rudolph-red-nosed-reindeer (Smithsonian)
F11. Montgomery Ward transferred the Rudolph copyright to May (1946/47).
   - https://archive.dartmouthalumnimagazine.com/article/1947/10/1/rudolph

## Black Friday
F12. "Black Friday" was coined by Philadelphia police in the early 1960s for the traffic jams; merchants tried to rename it "Big Friday"; the "into the black / profit" explanation appears later (earliest ~1981).
   - https://www.vocabulary.com/cm/wordroutes/the-origins-of-black-friday/
   - https://www.npr.org/transcripts/142767700

## Value of gifts
F16. Joel Waldfogel (economist, Wharton; paper "The Deadweight Loss of Christmas", American Economic Review, 1993): people value gifts they receive ~20% less than items they buy themselves; ~$13 billion of value lost per year in the US (his estimate, critics dispute it).
   - https://www.pbs.org/newshour/economy/business-july-dec09-scroogenomics_12-24
   - https://macleans.ca/culture/the-only-good-xmas-present/

## Debt
F18. LendingTree (survey Dec 10–15, 2025, 2,032 consumers): 37% took on holiday debt, average $1,223 (highest since 2022); 41% of them still paying the previous year's holiday debt; 40% paying 20%+ interest; 63% expect 3+ months to pay off; 45% of gift-givers used BNPL at least once.
   - https://www.lendingtree.com/credit-cards/study/holiday-debt-tariffs/
F19. NY Fed: Q4 2025 credit card balances rose $44 billion to a record $1.28 trillion. Q1 2026: fell $25 billion to $1.25 trillion (seasonal).
   - https://www.newyorkfed.org/newsevents/news/research/2026/20260210
   - https://www.newyorkfed.org/newsevents/news/research/2026/20260512
F20. Fed G.19: average APR on credit card accounts assessed interest 22.36% (Aug 2026).
   - https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/TERMCBCCINTNS

## Our own calculation (authorial)
C1. $1,223 balance, 22.36% APR, minimum payment = interest + 1% of balance, floor $25 (common issuer formula), no new charges: 91 monthly payments (~7.6 years), total interest ≈ $1,203. If payments start January 2026, the last one is around July 2033. Caveat: worst case; LendingTree 2024 found ~20% of holiday debtors make only minimum payments.
   - formula: https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/how-is-my-minimum-credit-card-payment-calculated-en-48/
   - (the 20% figure: https://lendingtree.com/credit-cards/study/average-holiday-debt , 2023 survey)
C2. Q4 2025 vs Q1 2026: +$44B on, −$25B off → ~$19B of the quarter's rise still there at end of March (net figures, not only holiday purchases).
