# T04 — They Advertised $146. The Checkout Said $649. — сценарий (версия 2, после фактчекера)
Окраска: детективная игра со зрителем («найди $273 на экране») + сатира как приправа (бейдж «NO HIDDEN FEES», «tax recovery charges», «5% off»). Шутки — сухие колкости в адрес схемы; сотрудники поддержки и владелец — не мишень, только факты из иска.
Сквозная линия: один заказ из иска, экран за экраном. Предмет-талисман: красная кнопка Continue (и бейдж NO HIDDEN FEES — возвращается в финале).
Структура: холодный вход ($146 → $649, иск, игра) → прогулка по экранам → перемотка и прокрутка: строка под кнопкой, наш расчёт, принцип к ~2:00 → «налоги» без налога → самое сильное на ~6:00: они знали с 2022, групповые иски, FTC, сотни миллионов → AutoRefill → как не попасться → финал к бейджу.
Строки `> SEE:` — что видим (не озвучиваются). Fig. N — скриншоты из иска FTC (подпись «Source: FTC v. Lens.com complaint»).

## PART 1

In February twenty twenty-six, a contact lens website advertised a full year of lenses for one hundred forty-six dollars. The checkout total said six hundred forty-nine. [PAUSE]
> SEE: Fig. 1 — реклама Google $18.29 → резкая склейка: Fig. 12 — Order Total $649.71; обещание обложки

No hacker. No glitch. Every screen of that checkout is in a federal lawsuit. This October, the Federal Trade Commission, Utah and Nevada sued the seller, Lens.com.
> SEE: первая страница иска (номер дела 2:26-cv-03232); скриншот пресс-релиза FTC с маркером

About fifty million Americans wear contacts. So let's play a game. I'll take you through the exact screens a shopper saw. Somewhere along the way, two hundred seventy-three dollars joins the order. It's on the page the whole time. Try to spot it before I do.
> SEE: титр «FIND THE $273.44»; лупа

Ready? Let's go shopping.

## PART 3

So let's rewind to the shipping page. This time, don't click Continue. Scroll down.
> SEE: перемотка назад; Fig. 8 → прокрутка вниз → Fig. 10

Under the button sits an order summary. Subtotal, three sixty-six thirty-two. Shipping, nine ninety-five. And one more line. Taxes and fees. Two hundred seventy-three dollars and forty-four cents. [PAUSE]
> SEE: Fig. 10–11 — маркер на «Taxes & fees $273.44»

That's where it was. Under the button you were told to press.
> SEE: стрелка от Continue вниз к строке

The lawsuit says shoppers who just clicked Continue never saw that line at all. One forty-six thirty-two, plus the fee, plus shipping. Four twenty-nine seventy-one. To the cent.
> SEE: столбик 146.32 + 273.44 + 9.95 = 429.71; «our calculation»

And that's the number after the rebate. Submit the order, and according to the lawsuit, the full six forty-nine seventy-one is what gets charged. The two hundred twenty dollar rebate comes by mail. You send in a form and wait for a prepaid card.
> SEE: Fig. 12 — строка rebate −$220; конверт, почтовый ящик; подпись про prepaid card «Source: lens.com FAQ»

Now let's do the math the ad didn't. The lowest price was eighteen twenty-nine a box. Split the fee across eight boxes and it's thirty-four eighteen a box. That's almost twice the advertised price of the box it's attached to. [PAUSE]
> SEE: две колонки: «LENSES $18.29» и «FEE $34.18»; «our calculation»

Even after the rebate, every box cost about fifty-two forty-seven before shipping. Nearly three times the price on Google.
> SEE: $18.29 → $52.47; «our calculation»

According to the lawsuit, that's the pattern. Show the cheapest slice of the price up front and collect the rest after the shopper has done all the work. By then you've typed in your prescription and your doctor's name. Nobody wants to do that twice.
> SEE: заполненные формы складываются стопкой

And the FTC says that's the damage. If you never see the real total, it's much harder to compare it with anybody else's.
> SEE: Fig. 1 — цены других продавцов в блоке Google размываются

But the name of that line raises a bigger question. Taxes and fees. Whose taxes?
> SEE: крупно слово «Taxes» в строке

## PART 4

Next to the charge there's a little gray "i." Click it, and the site explains. The taxes part is tax recovery charges. The fees part pays for servicing your order.
> SEE: Fig. 14 — поп-ап, маркер на «tax recovery charges»

Most people read taxes as sales tax. Here's the problem. Alaska, Delaware, Montana, New Hampshire and Oregon have no state sales tax. And according to the lawsuit, Florida, New Jersey and Virginia exempt contact lenses. Shoppers in all eight states got the taxes and fees line anyway.
> SEE: карта США — восемь штатов подсвечиваются по очереди

A shopper in New Hampshire pointed out there's no sales tax in their state. Customer service said the fees couldn't be removed.
> SEE: цитата из иска ¶83 на экране

A shopper in Florida was billed two hundred forty-one dollars and ninety-two cents in taxes and fees. A state that, the lawsuit says, doesn't tax contacts.
> SEE: Флорида на карте; сумма $241.92

And in Virginia, a rep wrote this. We are not charging customers sales taxes or state taxes. [PAUSE]
> SEE: цитата на экране с многоточием: «We are not charging customers sales taxes or state taxes…», «Source: FTC complaint ¶83»

So whose taxes? According to the lawsuit, none of it is sales tax. One class action from twenty twenty-four goes further. It claims the whole line was a processing fee, and that the only way to see it spelled out was to ask customer service for a full receipt.
> SEE: квитанция с надписью «FULL RECEIPT — on request»

One rep also said the fee is charged per box. If that's right, the bigger the order, the bigger the fee. And a year's supply is the biggest order there is.
> SEE: 1 коробка → 8 коробок, счётчик сбора растёт

Customers noticed. According to the lawsuit, Lens.com's own records hold thousands of complaints. One shopper asked how an order went from one forty-six to two forty-five. Another said the price had doubled. The reply? The fees are not hidden.
> SEE: стопка жалоб; две цитаты из ¶81

If you want more checkouts taken apart screen by screen, subscribe. It's free. No fees under the button.
> SEE: кнопка подписки; под ней пусто

Now, a confusing checkout can be an accident. According to the lawsuit, this one didn't go unnoticed inside the company.
> SEE: календарь листается назад к 2022

## PART 5

August thirty-first, twenty twenty-two. An email goes out with a PowerPoint attached. It's about what the lawsuit calls the current checkout process.
> SEE: реконструкция письма с вложением, подпись «illustration», не оригинал; дата 08/31/2022

According to the lawsuit, that presentation said it plainly. The total cost only showed up after scrolling below the Continue button. And it proposed showing the fees more clearly.
> SEE: на «слайде» — схема экрана, строка под кнопкой обведена

It went to the owner, Cary Samourkachian. The lawsuit says he's the only owner, the only officer and the only employee of Lens.com. Customer service runs through another company he owns, Speed Commerce, and an outside call center in the Philippines.
> SEE: схема: Lens.com — Samourkachian — Speed Commerce — колл-центр

So after that slide, what changed? According to the FTC, nothing that mattered. [PAUSE]
> SEE: красная кнопка Continue; поверх — годы 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026

Then came the lawsuits. Starting in twenty twenty-four, shoppers sued. By twenty twenty-five, five class actions against Lens.com sat in one Nevada federal court. In one case, Lens.com argued that the fee was disclosed before checkout, and any reasonable shopper would notice the total change.
> SEE: скриншот приказа о консолидации (май 2025) с маркером «Five separate cases»

In October twenty twenty-four, a federal judge in Nevada let that case keep going. This April, a federal judge in Florida said whether that line is deceptive is a question for an actual jury.
> SEE: скриншоты статей FKKS и Bloomberg Law с маркерами

In August twenty twenty-five, the FTC sent the company a formal demand for information. According to the lawsuit, the practices kept going.
> SEE: штамп «CIVIL INVESTIGATIVE DEMAND — AUG 2025»

And the screens you just clicked through? The lawsuit's screenshots were captured on February twenty-fourth, twenty twenty-six. Same red button. Same line underneath. [PAUSE]
> SEE: Fig. 8 и Fig. 10 рядом; дата 24.02.2026

More than four years of this, the lawsuit says. And it says the company made hundreds of millions of dollars.
> SEE: счётчик крутится; титр «hundreds of millions — FTC complaint»

The same low prices also sold a subscription. AutoRefill. Subscribe and save, five percent off every future order. According to the lawsuit, the taxes and fees weren't clearly disclosed before customers handed over their card.
> SEE: Fig. 15–16 — «Save 5% OFF», «YOU'RE SUBSCRIBED»

Five percent off. In our February order, the fee alone was about three quarters of the subtotal.
> SEE: «−5%» рядом с «+75%»; «our calculation»

And according to the lawsuit, how to cancel wasn't in the checkout either. It sat on a separate page under Help. Some customers who tried to cancel by chat were told to call.
> SEE: реконструкция меню Help внизу страницы (illustration); телефонная трубка

## PART 6

To be clear, the FTC's case is a lawsuit, not a verdict. Lens.com hasn't been found liable, and the court hasn't ruled on it.
> SEE: здание суда

Until it does, here's how to stop paying for the space under a button.
> SEE: красная кнопка Continue, под ней пустое место

Before you click Continue on any checkout, scroll all the way down. If the total lives below the button, that's your answer.
> SEE: палец прокручивает страницу на телефоне

Compare totals, not box prices. Take the same cart to the final screen on two sites and compare what your card will actually be charged.
> SEE: два телефона рядом, два итога

A price after rebate is a price after you mail a form and wait. Your card pays the full amount the day you order.
> SEE: конверт; календарь недель

And you can shop anywhere. Under federal law, your eye doctor has to give you a copy of your contact lens prescription after the fitting, even if you don't ask.
> SEE: рецепт на линзы в руке; «FTC Contact Lens Rule»

If you were charged for something you never saw, call your card issuer, and report it at reportfraud dot ftc dot gov.

Remember why customers choose Lens.com? No hidden fees.
