ABC News checked one Michael Kors purse in a T.J. Maxx. The tag said compare at three twenty-eight. The maker's suggested price was two twenty-eight.

T.J. Maxx settled, and a judge approved it in twenty eighteen. It agreed to change how it explains Compare At and to put up more signs. The comparison prices are still on the tags.

So why does a made-up number work on smart people? Because nobody judges a price alone. We judge it next to something. One sixty-nine next to four ninety-nine feels like a win. Next to ninety-five twenty, it feels like a mistake.

Even the drive is part of it. Checkbook says outlet centers were usually built at least an hour from big cities, so they wouldn't pull full-price shoppers out of the local malls. And after an hour on the highway, who drives home with nothing?
