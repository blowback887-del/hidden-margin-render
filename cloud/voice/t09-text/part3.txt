In another lawsuit filed this summer, shoppers sued the company behind Tommy Hilfiger. Their lawyers hired a textile expert to compare clothes from Macy's with clothes from a Tommy Hilfiger outlet. Look at the polo shirts, as the lawsuit describes them.

The Macy's polo was ninety-six percent cotton. Three buttons. The outlet polo was sixty percent cotton and forty percent polyester. Two buttons. Made in a different country.

Same name on the chest. According to the lawsuit, a different shirt.

Checkbook found the same pattern again and again. At Banana Republic Factory, a suit jacket for a hundred and forty dollars, with no lining. At the regular store, a partly lined jacket, three hundred and fifty.

Cole Haan outlet dress shoes had rubber soles. The department store pair had leather. A Gap Factory barn jacket, fifty dollars. The one at the regular Gap, a hundred and forty-eight, with more buttons and more tailoring.

So the outlet isn't selling you the expensive thing for cheap. It's selling you a cheaper thing, with the expensive thing's name on it. And the tag points at the expensive one. [PAUSE]

Here's how to catch it before you pay. Look at the label inside. A J.Crew Factory label has two little diamonds under the name. Banana Republic Factory has three. A Talbots outlet label is white, with three dots. At Brooks Brothers, a label that just says eighteen eighteen is outlet. Established eighteen eighteen is the regular store.

Then read the fabric line. If the outlet version is mostly polyester and the mall version is cotton, you now know what the discount was for.
