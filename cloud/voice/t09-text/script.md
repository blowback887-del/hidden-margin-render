# T09 — Why the Outlet Sells a $499 Bag for $169 — сценарий (версия 3, длинная, ~13,5 мин, после фактчекера)
Окраска: детектив «кто написал на бирке $499» + сухая сатира на язык цен. Ставки растут от одной сумки к целой стране и к нам самим.
Сквозная линия: одна бирка Kate Spade Outlet «comparable value $499 / $169» (иск 2026). Расследование: бирка → вещь (сшита для аутлета) → цифра (кто её пишет) → все магазины (иски) → развязка: единственный CEO, который убрал фальшивые цены, и что с ним стало (JCPenney 2012). Ответ на главный вопрос: цифра на бирке существует, потому что работает на нас.
Отложенное обещание: в ~1:30 обещаем «человека, который попробовал это прекратить» — раскрываем на ~10:00.
Одна фраза: магазины пишут на бирках цену, которой не было, потому что честную цену покупатели наказали рублём — и вот как видеть это насквозь.
Хит-образец (Costco-куртки 2 млн; Business Decoded 148K): бирка в первом кадре и обе цены за 5 с; миф «это остатки» ломается к ~2:30; метка на этикетке — первый практический ход. Наше отличие: улики из исков 2026, расследование одной бирки и поворот с JCPenney.
Под ТВ и зрителя 55+: обе суммы — вслух, цифра держится ≥3 с, одна цифра на кадр.
Строки `> SEE:` — что видим (не озвучиваются).

## PART 2

Let's start with the bag itself. Most of us have a story in our heads about outlets. It's last season's stuff. The leftovers. The color nobody bought.
> SEE: склад, коробки, вешалки с остатками (сток)

And that story used to be closer to the truth. In twenty fourteen, four members of Congress wrote to the Federal Trade Commission. Historically, they wrote, outlets sold excess inventory and slightly damaged goods.
> SEE: скриншот письма 2014 года на сайте сенатора Whitehouse; выделено «excess inventory and slightly damaged goods»

But do the math on one brand. J.Crew has about a hundred regular stores. And three hundred and thirty Factory stores. [PAUSE]
> SEE: столбики: «~100 J.Crew» против «330 J.Crew Factory»

That's more than three outlets for every real store. You can't fill three stores with the leftovers of one.
> SEE: одна вешалка «leftovers» против трёх полных магазинов

So where does it all come from? That same letter said some analysts estimated upwards of eighty-five percent of outlet merchandise was made only for outlets.
> SEE: письмо, выделено «upwards of 85%»; плашка «upwards of 85% made for outlets · analysts' estimate, 2014»

That was almost thirteen years ago. This year, a nonprofit called Consumers' Checkbook went to see for itself. Four researchers. Three months. Forty brands.
> SEE: отчёт Checkbook (checkbook.org, июнь 2026); плашка «4 researchers · 3 months · 40 brands»

They found that more than half of what's sold at outlet and factory stores was made for those stores. J.Crew Factory. Banana Republic Factory. Gap Factory. Ann Taylor Factory. And Kate Spade sells a mix.
> SEE: список брендов из отчёта; Kate Spade подсвечен «mix»

Checkbook emailed all forty companies to ask how it works. One answered. [PAUSE]
> SEE: 40 конвертов, один ответ — Nike; плашка «1 of 40 replied»

Some don't even hide it. Gap's own website says its factory stores don't sell overstock or last season's trends. Ann Taylor's site says its LOFT Outlet pieces are created specifically for outlets. They just don't put that on the tag.

So if the bag was made for the outlet, the next question is simple. Made how?

## PART 4

Now the big number. Who actually writes it?
> SEE: пустая бирка, ручка пишет «$499»

In twenty fourteen, a shopper named Tressa Gattinella sued Michael Kors over a pair of pants from its outlet. The tag said MSRP, a hundred and twenty dollars. Our price, seventy-nine ninety-nine.
> SEE: бирка «MSRP $120 · Our price $79.99» (реконструкция по иску, плашка «as described in the lawsuit»)

MSRP means manufacturer's suggested retail price. Her lawsuit said those pants were made only for the outlet. So the price was suggested for a store where they were never sold.
> SEE: «M-S-R-P» по буквам; пустая витрина бутика

Michael Kors denied doing anything wrong. But in twenty fifteen it agreed to pay four point eight eight million dollars to settle. And it agreed to change the tags.
> SEE: заголовок Reuters о мировом; сумма $4.88M держится

Here's how. It would stop using the letters MSRP. And replace them with one word. Value. Or drop the big number on outlet-only items. [PAUSE]
> SEE: на бирке «MSRP» зачёркивается, вписывается «VALUE»

A new word for the same number.
> SEE: две бирки рядом, отличаются одним словом

Eleven years later, look at the tag on our bag again. Comparable value.
> SEE: возврат к бирке Kate Spade, «COMPARABLE VALUE» подсвечено

Comparable to what? In Oregon, the rules say a compared price should come from a real competitor in the same area, selling similar goods. The lawsuit says Kate Spade sets that number the same way across the whole country. And it points to a competitor in the same outlet center charging ninety-five twenty for a similar tote. [PAUSE]
> SEE: текст правила Орегона (раздел иска о законе); карта центра: KSO и Michael Kors Outlet; $95.20

Same story with a wallet from that store. Comparable value, two thirty-nine. After an extra twenty-five percent off, fifty-nine twenty-five. And the lawsuit says a similar leather wallet at Michael Kors in the same center was forty-seven twenty.
> SEE: страница иска ¶45–47; бирка кошелька «$239 → $59.25», рядом «$47.20»

It gets stranger. Checkbook found a Banana Republic Factory shirtdress with a compare at price of one forty. The real cotton dress at the regular store was one ninety-eight. So the number on the tag matched neither dress.
> SEE: три бирки: «Factory $75», «compare at $140», «mainline $198»; знак вопроса над $140

And the Tommy Hilfiger lawsuit goes further. The lawyers say they checked California outlet stores on and off from twenty twenty-two to twenty twenty-five. And not once did they see a product offered at its full original price. Hundreds, maybe thousands, of products, marked on sale the whole time. [PAUSE]
> SEE: календарь листается 2022 → 2025; табличка «SALE» не снимается; цитата из иска

A sale that never ends isn't a sale. It's just the price.

At this point you might think, fine, outlets are a special case. Stay away from outlet malls and you're safe. [PAUSE]
> SEE: машина проезжает мимо указателя «Premium Outlets»

Except the same number lives in the stores you shop at every week.

Years before the outlet lawsuits, a man named Antonio Hinojos took Kohl's to court. He'd bought a Samsonite suitcase marked fifty percent off its original price of two ninety-nine ninety-nine. His claim was that Kohl's routinely sold that merchandise at the sale price.
> SEE: чемодан, бирка «ORIGINAL $299.99 · 50% OFF»; заголовок о деле Hinojos v. Kohl's

A lower court threw the case out. It said he got what he wanted, at the price advertised, so where's the harm? [PAUSE]
> SEE: молоток судьи; плашка «Dismissed»

In twenty thirteen, the federal appeals court disagreed. To shoppers, it said, the original price matters. Or in the court's own short words: price advertisements matter.
> SEE: страница решения 9-го округа 2013 года; выделено «price advertisements matter»

In December twenty sixteen, the city of Los Angeles sued four of the biggest names in American retail. J.C. Penney. Kohl's. Macy's. And Sears.
> SEE: заголовки декабря 2016 года; четыре вывески по очереди

One example from the lawsuit against Sears. A washing machine advertised with an original price of eleven seventy-nine ninety-nine. The city said that in roughly its first six months online, Sears never offered it for more than nine ninety-nine ninety-nine.
> SEE: стиральная машина; бирка «ORIGINAL $1,179.99», рядом «never more than $999.99 online»

So, according to the city, the original price was higher than any price it actually had. [PAUSE]
> SEE: график: линия цены не дотягивает до потолка «original»

Or take Macy's. The city said a cross pendant necklace went online with an original price of a hundred and twenty dollars, and a sale price of thirty. Seventy-five percent off, from the day it went on sale. And in the five months after, it never sold for more than thirty. [PAUSE]

Off-price stores have their own version. T.J. Maxx puts a comparison price on its tags. When shoppers sued in twenty fifteen, its own website described that number as its buying staff's estimate.

## PART 6

In twenty eleven, J.C. Penney hired a new CEO. Ron Johnson. He came from Apple. He was the man behind the Apple Store.
> SEE: архивное фото магазина J.C. Penney; плашка «Ron Johnson · CEO · former Apple retail chief»

Johnson looked at J.C. Penney and saw a store hooked on fake prices. Tags marked up, then marked down. Coupons, one sale after another. So in early twenty twelve, J.C. Penney promised to end what it called fake prices. One everyday price on the tag. Johnson called it fair and square.
> SEE: реклама JCPenney 2012 «fair and square» (архив); ворох купонов летит в мусор

On paper, this is exactly what we've been asking for this whole video. A store that tells you the real price. [PAUSE]
> SEE: бирка с одной цифрой, без зачёркнутой цены

The customers left.

Johnson admitted the pricing was a mistake. And he explained why in one sentence. The customer, he said, loves a sale, at times she loves a coupon, and she always wants a reference price.

The sales and the coupons came back. And according to reports, J.C. Penney quietly raised its everyday prices, so it would have something to mark down. One side table went from a hundred and fifty dollars to a two forty-five everyday price within days.

Johnson was out after about seventeen months.

A Northwestern marketing professor put it this way at the time. People don't want a fair price. They want a great deal.

And in December twenty sixteen, J.C. Penney was one of the four stores Los Angeles sued over original prices. [PAUSE]

## PART 7

Why does a made-up number work on smart people? Because nobody judges a price alone. We judge it next to something. One sixty-nine next to four ninety-nine feels like a win. Next to ninety-five twenty, it feels like a mistake.
> SEE: та же цифра $169 рядом с $499 (зелёная) и рядом с $95.20 (красная)

Even the drive is part of it. Checkbook says outlet centers were usually built at least an hour from big cities, so they wouldn't pull full-price shoppers out of the local malls. And after an hour on the highway, who drives home with nothing?
> SEE: трасса, указатель «Premium Outlets»; парковка, полные пакеты

To be fair, real bargains do exist. Checkbook found that some stores really sell leftovers. A Burberry outlet shirt with a seven hundred dollar original price, marked down to two fifty. Scratched dressers at an RH outlet, eighty percent off.
> SEE: Burberry outlet (фото), бирка «$700 → $250»; поцарапанный комод с наклейкой −80%

Those are the real thing, just late. And they often look messy. At Nike clearance stores, Checkbook saw stickers slapped on by hand, with the date the item came in or got marked down. A real markdown often looks like a sticker on an old price, not a neat printed tag with two numbers.
> SEE: рука клеит ценник-стикер поверх старой цены; дата на стикере

So next time, three moves. Thumb over the big number. Check the label inside, for the code and the fabric. And take ten seconds with your phone, because Checkbook often found Adidas cheaper at Amazon or Dick's than at the Adidas outlet.
> SEE: три иконки по очереди: палец на бирке, этикетка, телефон с поиском

Now back to our bag. One sixty-nine might be a fair price for it. It might not. But it was never a four hundred ninety-nine dollar bag on sale. It's a one sixty-nine bag with a story written on its tag. [PAUSE]
> SEE: бирка Kate Spade, $499 тает, остаётся $169

The stores will keep writing that story, because it works. What you can change is whether you believe it.
