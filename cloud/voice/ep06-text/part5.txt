And the ad is slowly shrinking. Government forecasters expect Americans to buy less fuel this year and next. Cars keep going farther on every gallon. So gas stations are quietly turning into something else. Restaurants with a really big parking lot. Most stations stop at hot dogs and coffee. But one chain took this idea all the way. And it started with one station in a town of about twelve thousand people. Nineteen sixty-eight. Boone, Iowa. A guy named Don Lamberti buys a tiny three-bay service station. The idea came from his fuel supplier, Kurvin Fish. Don turns the station into a little store and names it after his friend's initials. K.C. Casey's. It works. He opens another. And another. In small towns other chains didn't bother with. Then, in nineteen eighty-four, Casey's tries something weird. Pizza. Made in the store. At a gas station. That sounds like a dare. It became the plan. By the end of the next year, pizza was in eighty-five stores. Later came a breakfast pizza. With sausage gravy. At a gas station. In Iowa, this is considered a balanced breakfast. Today Casey's has about two thousand nine hundred and fifty stores in nineteen states. Nearly half of them in towns of five thousand people or fewer. And by sales, Bank of America's analysts rank Casey's the fifth-biggest pizza chain in America.
