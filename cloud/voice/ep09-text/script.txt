## ROUND 2 — The Line (1:50–3:30)

VO: But Dale has to walk to the steak. And that walk is not an accident.
SEE: Top-down map of a buffet line, dotted path from the door to the grill at the far end.

VO: Golden Corral's CEO told The Wall Street Journal the buffet is built in what he calls modules. Every module pairs something expensive with something cheap. Shrimp sits with hush puppies and french fries. The Journal's own example: meatloaf, flanked by potatoes, gravy and green beans. Nothing is hidden. It's just arranged.
SEE: Source line: WSJ, via Fox News, Oct 2026. Map zooms into "modules": shrimp tray flanked by fries and hush puppies; each tray gets a small $ or ¢ badge.

VO: Translation from corporate: nothing expensive is ever left alone without a chaperone made of potato.
SEE: GAG — subtitle "translated from Corporate"; a potato in sunglasses standing next to a shrimp like a bodyguard.

VO: The Hustle once priced out a buffet plate with wholesale data. A serving of steak: about two dollars and twenty-five cents. A serving of potatoes: thirty cents.
SEE: Two plates side by side with price tags $2.25 / $0.30 (The Hustle estimate). Scale tips.

VO: Beef has only gotten pricier since. Potatoes are still potatoes.
SEE: Steak tag climbs with an arrow; potato tag shrugs.

VO: So every scoop of mashed potatoes Dale adds, just as a side, swaps a two-dollar bite for a thirty-cent one. And nobody makes him do it. He does it to himself. With a ladle. Smiling.
SEE: Picture-by-word: plate fills, a running "cost to the house" counter barely moves on potatoes. GAG — stock: guy happily piling mashed potatoes.

VO: Then there's the roll. Warm, shiny, basically a pillow with butter on it. It's one of the cheapest things on the line, and it takes up exactly the space Dale was saving for steak number three.
SEE: TALISMAN — hero shot of a dinner roll, slow push-in, choir sting. Stomach diagram: roll occupies the "steak #3" slot.

VO: And here's a twist I didn't expect. At a buffet, bad food is expensive. If a dish is dry, you don't send it back. You dump it and grab something else, and the house pays twice. So the cheap stuff actually has to be good. Those potatoes are a weapon. They have to taste like one.
SEE: Stock: plate scraped into a bin (coral "−$" pops). Then glossy mashed potatoes with butter, hero lighting. GAG — caption "weapon-grade".

## ROUND 3 — The Decoy (3:30–5:00)

## ROUND 4 — The Average (5:00–7:10)

VO: Still, let's give Dale his moment. He pushes through. Four plates. Steak on every single one. Say he eats twenty-five dollars' worth of food on a nineteen-dollar ticket. He beat the house. [PAUSE]

VO: By six dollars. And the house does not care. Because the house was never playing against Dale.
SEE: Scoreboard zooms out: Dale's lamp is one tiny light in a wall of hundreds.

VO: Here's the number that runs the whole place. Per the Journal, about thirty-eight cents of every dollar Golden Corral takes in goes to food. On an eighteen ninety-nine dinner, that's about seven dollars a head. Not Dale's head. The average head.

VO: And look who's in the average. A typical Golden Corral feeds more than six thousand people a week. That's around nine hundred a day. The Hustle's estimate is that maybe one in twenty is a Dale. So tonight, forty-five Dales.

SEE: Scoreboard flickers. Music drops to a single low note.

VO: Not because of Dale. Because of the regulars. The chain's most loyal guests come about seventy times a year. And the CEO says one regular put it to him like this: instead of five or six visits a month, it's two.
