By the summer of twenty twenty-five, McDonald's had a problem it couldn't ignore. And the fix it came up with is the best trick in this whole story.

In September, McDonald's announced that Extra Value Meals were back. Eight combos, priced to save you fifteen percent compared to buying the sandwich, fries and drink separately. There was even an eight-dollar Big Mac Meal to celebrate.

The Wall Street Journal reported that McDonald's would help cover the cost for franchise owners who opted in. Remember that the next time someone tells you the board price is set by the market.

Headlines called it McDonald's cutting prices. Sounds great. [PAUSE]

But fifteen percent off of what? Off the separate prices. The same prices that went up about forty percent after Extra Value Meals disappeared in twenty nineteen.

Let's do the math with their own averages. Take an average price in twenty nineteen and call it a dollar. Add forty percent. A dollar forty. Now take off the fifteen percent "savings." You're at about a dollar nineteen. [PAUSE]

So even with the new discount, the combo works out to roughly nineteen percent above what the separate items cost in twenty nineteen. Using their own averages. And the eight-dollar Big Mac Meal? That was a limited-time offer. Prices vary by location.

That's the move. Raise the board. Remove the discount. Then sell the discount back, measured from the new price, and call it value.
