Remember the Extra Value Meal? Pick a number, get a sandwich, fries and a drink, and pay less than buying them one by one. That was the whole deal. The meal was the discount.

In twenty nineteen, McDonald's quietly dropped Extra Value Meals as a menu category. No funeral. No announcement you'd remember. It just faded off the board.

Then came the pandemic, then inflation, and prices on the board kept climbing. By last year, the CEO, Chris Kempczinski, said it out loud on a call with investors. Customers were, quote, "too often" seeing combo meals that cost more than ten dollars.

He also said the single biggest thing that shapes what people think of McDonald's value… is the menu board. [PAUSE]

The menu board. The thing franchise owners had been raising for five years.

And the people who noticed first were the people who could least afford it. In late twenty twenty-five, Kempczinski told investors that across fast food, visits from lower-income customers had been falling by nearly double digits. For almost two years. Visits from higher earners were growing about as fast.

Think about who that is. The guy who stops for lunch between two jobs. The grandparent who used to take the kids out on a Saturday because it was cheap. That was McDonald's whole business. And they were walking away.

People said it plainly. One customer wrote: "Buy one, get one for a dollar is gone. So am I."
