Because it's not the groceries. Imagine a mom named Dana. Saturday morning. Full cart. One kid negotiating for a cereal that's mostly marshmallows. At the register she does one tiny thing, without even thinking. Remember it. It matters more than anything in her cart. And Dana is not a careless shopper. She compares prices. She clips coupons. She once drove across town to save a dollar on detergent, which, honestly, cost her more in gas. The total comes to one hundred dollars. Dana feels robbed. Fair. But let's follow that hundred dollars. At a chain like Kroger, about seventy-seven of it goes to the food itself, and getting it onto the shelf. That leaves twenty-three. Now pay the cashier, the stocker, the rent, the freezers humming all night, and everything that walks out the door without paying. What's left for the store? About two dollars.
