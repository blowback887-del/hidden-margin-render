# T02 — The Free Sample That Costs $5,000 — сценарий (версия 4: после фактчекера 18.11.2026)
Окраска: детективное расследование (улики по шагам, «кто за этим стоит») + триллер (ставка растёт от $5,000 до схемы, которую уже ловили федералы). Юмор — сухие колкости, 4–5 на ролик: среди пострадавших пожилые и люди с инвалидностью, поэтому смеёмся только над схемой и её языком.
Сквозная линия: Мишель Анликер распутывает историю мамы → мы идём дальше неё. Предмет-талисман: пакетик-пробник.
Структура: холодный вход ($5,000 → $42,000 → $44,280) → «рецепт» в пять шагов (ответ на название к ~3:30) → поворот «это не один магазин» → кто за этим стоит → самое сильное на ~6:30: это уже ловили 10+ лет назад → новый адрес той же компании → правила.
Строки `> SEE:` — что видим (не озвучиваются).

## PART 1

It starts with a free skincare sample at the mall. You've walked past these kiosks a hundred times.
> SEE: крупно ладонь берёт крошечный пакетик-пробник; киоск в ТЦ, продавец протягивает пробник

In Ann Arbor, Michigan, one woman walked out of a store like this with five thousand dollars of skincare she never wanted.
> SEE: титр «FREE» на пакетике зачёркивается → «$5,000» (обещание обложки); пакет с покупками

And honestly? She got off easy. [PAUSE]
> SEE: стоп-кадр, музыка обрывается

Also in Michigan, an elderly man with dementia was charged forty-two thousand dollars for skincare. His family only found out when his credit score dropped.
> SEE: скриншот заголовка NPR «$42,000»; графика — шкала кредитного рейтинга падает

In Santa Fe, a sixty-seven year old woman was kept in a store for hours until she agreed to spend six hundred dollars. Later, her banking app showed two charges. Forty-four thousand, two hundred and eighty.
> SEE: телефон, банковское приложение: $600 → две строки, $44,280 (реконструкция, подпись «illustration»)

NPR spent a year on stories like these. More than fifty people, nearly five hundred complaints. And the trail kept pointing to one company.

So how does a free sample turn into a five thousand dollar bill? And if federal agents documented a credit card overcharge scheme back in twenty thirteen, why is the same business model still running today?

## PART 2

NPR heard the same story again and again, from people who never met. It works in steps. Like a recipe.
> SEE: титр «THE RECIPE» мелко сбоку; шаг 1 появляется в углу

Step one. The sample. A young, friendly salesperson steps out of a kiosk with a little packet. A compliment on your handbag. The promise of younger looking skin.
> SEE: сток: продавец у киоска, пробник, улыбки; лёгкий замедленный кадр

Why does that work? Reciprocity. Someone gives you something, your brain says you owe them. NPR once reported on waiters who gave diners a second mint and said it was just for them. Their tips went up twenty percent. One extra mint. [PAUSE]
> SEE: мятная конфетка на блюдце со счётом; «+20%» мелко внизу

Now imagine a free facial.
> SEE: кресло для процедур под лампой, пустое

Step two. The chair. If you stop, you're walked inside. Some customers say they were physically pulled in. Then you sit. People describe sitting for an hour or more, sometimes in a back room.
> SEE: сток: дверь в подсобку закрывается; часы на стене ускоренно

In Hawaii, a woman with a disability went in for a free facial. She told the state the salesperson took her walker and wouldn't let her leave for hours, until she spent two thousand six hundred dollars.
> SEE: ходунки у стены, камера медленно отъезжает; документ-жалоба (стилизованный, «illustration»)

Step three. The price. Look at the shelf. Notice anything missing?
> SEE: полка с баночками крупным планом — ни одного ценника

No price tags. A former federal investigator who watched these sales up close told NPR why. No price tag means the salesperson charges whatever they think they can get. The more they sell, the higher their commission. One salesman bragged in a video that it can mean ten thousand dollars a month.
> SEE: цитата Matt Grimm титром; полка, лупа — ценников нет

So the first number is huge on purpose. One complaint says a shopper was offered a twenty thousand dollar package, and the price dropped every time they said no. Their words: I felt like I was in a car dealership.
> SEE: скриншот отзыва BBB (без имени); вставка — автосалон, продавец машет рукой

It's a discount. Off a number somebody made up thirty seconds ago. [PAUSE]
> SEE: мем: калькулятор, который считает сам по себе

## PART 3

Step four. The card. This is where hundreds become thousands.
> SEE: кредитная карта на прилавке, терминал

The Santa Fe woman was told her card was declined. So she handed over another one. That evening, she found two charges. Forty-four thousand dollars.
> SEE: терминал «DECLINED», рука достаёт вторую карту

Another shopper told authorities the salesperson simply added a zero. Thirteen hundred dollars became thirteen thousand.
> SEE: чек: «1,300» → появляется «0» → «13,000»

Some say their payment plan turned out to be a high interest loan.
> SEE: планшет с договором, мелкий шрифт крупно: «APR»

Step five. The bag. Customers say they signed a tablet without being told what it was. At the bottom of the bag, under the free gifts, the receipt. All sales final. No refunds. No exchanges.
> SEE: рука роется в пакете, достаёт чек; надпись «ALL SALES FINAL. NO REFUNDS. NO EXCHANGES.» крупно

The sample was free. The chair was not. [PAUSE]
> SEE: пакетик-пробник лежит на чеке

Michele did what most of us never do. She complained to the mall, filed a police report, handed out flyers, and found more than thirty people with complaints about that one store.

If you like seeing this stuff dragged into the light, subscribe.

The mall didn't fix it. What worked was going over its head, to Briarwood's owner, Simon Property Group. Only then did her mother get a refund.

## PART 4

In Manhattan Beach, California, there's a spa called Mia's by Voupre. Mary Ellen Eckhert, eighty two, a retired teacher in a motorized wheelchair, stopped for a free sample. Then she kept coming back for facials.
> SEE: побережье Манхэттен-Бич (сток), коляска у витрины спа

Over a year, she was charged fifty three thousand five hundred dollars on four credit cards. Twenty four thousand in one visit. After disputes, she's still out more than seventeen thousand. Her son asked the only question that matters. What kind of a spa charges anyone fifty four thousand dollars?
> SEE: четыре карты веером; выписка с красной строкой; цитата сына титром

Different state. Same five steps.
> SEE: три вывески рядом: Beauty Essentials / Santa Fe Spa Center by Voupre / Mia's by Voupre

And there's a clue hiding in plain sight. The name on the jars. Voupre.
> SEE: баночка Voupre крупно, логотип в фокусе

Michigan, New Mexico, California. Stores thousands of miles apart, which the company calls independently owned. All buying from one distributor. Mazal Group, of Chatsworth, California.
> SEE: карта — линии от трёх точек сходятся в Чатсуорт; здание из открытых источников

Court records show stores with a Mazal brand in their name must sell only Mazal products, follow its refund policy, and send complaints to headquarters. A former employee says it works with at least two thousand stores. Mazal's lawyer says the company does not own or operate any retail stores.
> SEE: страница судебного документа (стилизация), выделены три пункта; глобус с точками; цитата юриста титром

Now, what's in the jars? NPR got the price forms. One Voupre mask sells to the store for twenty three dollars. Suggested retail price, three thousand. That's a hundred and thirty times. A retail expert told NPR even luxury brands rarely go above twenty times.
> SEE: маска: «$23» → «$3,000», счётчик «×130»; рядом полоска «×20 luxury»

NPR also paid a lab to test a Voupre anti aging serum. Suggested price, thirteen hundred dollars. The label promises vitamin A and vitamin E. The lab found neither. One box, not the final word. But the lab's boss compared it to a product that sells for under ten dollars. [PAUSE]
> SEE: пробирки, лаборатория (сток); этикетка «Vitamin A, Vitamin E» → «NOT DETECTED»

It's not only Voupre. In Los Angeles, CBS investigated a store called Bee and Co. Victor Chisholm, a thirty year old man with an intellectual disability, has receipts showing about forty one thousand seven hundred dollars there. Skincare and a red light device. He says he was also pressured into sending employees more than ten thousand dollars in tips.
> SEE: кадр ТЦ Westfield Topanga; красная LED-маска; телефон с приложением оплаты (без имён); штамп «NO REFUNDS»

The store's lawyer calls the coverage one sided and blames a rogue employee for the tips. And the free sample packs from Bee and Co. listed an address in Chatsworth, California. [PAUSE]
> SEE: пакетик-пробник переворачивается — адрес «Chatsworth, CA» в фокусе

That location now appears closed. Los Angeles police are looking into his case.
> SEE: заклеенная витрина, замок-сейф на двери

## PART 5

Worst part? None of this is new. [PAUSE]
> SEE: архивная плёнка, год «2010»

In twenty thirteen, federal agents ran an operation code named Salty Winds. They watched skincare stores around Washington, made purchases, and documented what they called a credit card overcharge scheme.
> SEE: титр «OPERATION SALTY WINDS»; наблюдение за киоском через стекло машины

Another case went after a bigger chain on the East Coast, including Orogold kiosks. A brand owned by the same families behind Mazal Group. Its owner pleaded guilty to visa fraud and money laundering. His business had made over fourteen million dollars. Sentenced to six and a half years. He served thirty months.
> SEE: пресс-релиз Минюста 2016 (скриншот); «$14M+», «140+», «6.5 yrs → 30 mo»

An FBI agent told NPR the goal was to cut the head off the snake.
> SEE: цитата титром

The snake, it turns out, had licensees. [PAUSE]
> SEE: мем-пауза: карта, на которой точки снова загораются по всей стране

Today, NPR says, the same business model is running from Honolulu to Annapolis.
> SEE: карта США — точки от Гавайев до Мэриленда

The Santa Fe spa owner settled with New Mexico for three hundred fifty thousand dollars. In Hawaii, Mazal Group, the families and two local licensees agreed to pay nine hundred fifty thousand in a class action. They deny wrongdoing.
> SEE: документы исков; суммы мелко внизу

And a month before NPR published, Mazal Group's California business license was terminated. The sign on the Chatsworth headquarters now says INV Brands. Innovation Brands, founded in twenty twenty four by Mazal's owner and her husband. [PAUSE]
> SEE: здание в Чатсуорте, старая вывеска → новая

New name. Same address.
> SEE: табличка с адресом крупно

## PART 6

So. House rules.
> SEE: титр «HOUSE RULES», простая графика

One. The sample is a toll booth. Take it if you want, but keep walking. The trick starts the moment you stop.
> SEE: человек берёт пробник на ходу и идёт дальше

Two. No chair. No back room. Ever.
> SEE: пустое кресло, дверь в подсобку закрывается

Three. No price on the shelf? Get the total in writing before anything touches your face.
> SEE: рука показывает на пустую полку; ручка и листок

Four. Card declined? Don't reach for a second card. Leave. And check your banking app before you walk out.
> SEE: «DECLINED» → человек убирает кошелёк и уходит

Five. Don't count on three days to change your mind. The federal cooling off rule covers sales at your home or a temporary spot, not at a store's permanent location. Your real exit is your card company. Dispute fast.
> SEE: страница FTC (скриншот), выделено «permanent place of business»

And six. If your mom or dad walks the mall for exercise, glance at their statements now and then. That's how families here found out. A credit score. An empty bank account.
> SEE: взрослая дочь и пожилой отец за кухонным столом с выпиской

Michele's mom got her money back because her daughter refused to go away. A lot of people in this story never did.
