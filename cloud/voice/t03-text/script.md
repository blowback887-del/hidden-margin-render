# T03 — The Price Tag at Dollar General Isn't the Price — сценарий (версия 3, после фактчекера, 19.11.2026)
Окраска: детективное расследование (улики по шагам: одна жалоба → округ → штаты → федеральный иск) + сатира как приправа (табличка «All prices are final», «virtually impossible», магазин со словом «доллар» в названии). Шутки — сухие колкости, только в адрес компании и её системы; кассиры — не мишень.
Сквозная линия: жалоба Уильяма Андерсона из Огайо → куда она привела. Предмет-талисман: табличка у кассы «Prices cannot be changed at the register. All prices are final.»
Структура: холодный вход (Coffee Mate $2 → $4.35, табличка, иск FTC) → округ Батлер: все 20 магазинов провалены → ответ на название к ~2:00 (касса обновляется сама, ценник — руками) → «починили?» по штатам → самое сильное на ~6:00: кто платит и кто получает (меньше двух часов продаж) → тайные покупатели FTC → как не попасться → финал к табличке.
Строки `> SEE:` — что видим (не озвучиваются).

## PART 1

At a Dollar General in Butler County, Ohio, a Coffee Mate creamer had a price tag of two dollars. At the register, it rang up four thirty-five.
> SEE: крупно ценник на полке $2.00 → резкая склейка: экран кассы $4.35 (реконструкция, подпись «illustration»); обещание обложки

More than double. [PAUSE]
> SEE: титр «+117%» поверх, музыка обрывается

At another Dollar General in the same county, a shopper named William Anderson noticed a sign by the register. Prices cannot be changed at the register. All prices are final.
> SEE: табличка у кассы (реконструкция) с текстом из новости WCPO; маркер на строке «All prices are final»

Final. Just not the one on the shelf.
> SEE: ценник на полке медленно выцветает

Anderson wrote a complaint. It set off inspections, then a lawsuit in Ohio. And this October, the same problem landed in federal court. The Federal Trade Commission and twenty states are now suing Dollar General.
> SEE: скриншот статьи AP/ABC11 «Dollar General is sued by the FTC and 20 states» с маркером; карта США — 20 штатов загораются

Their lawsuit counts at least twenty-nine thousand, two hundred sixty-five shopper complaints in about three years. It also describes what happened when federal investigators went shopping undercover. We'll get to that.
> SEE: счётчик до 29,265; кадр — чек в руке; силуэт покупателя с пакетом, лицо в тени

So is this just a few lazy price tags? Or is it a system where the mistakes keep landing on one side of the counter?
> SEE: весы — чаша «shelf» вверху, «register» внизу

Let's follow the price tag.

## PART 2

Anderson's letter went to the office of Butler County's auditor, Roger Reynolds. His office runs the weights and measures inspectors. The people who check that a gallon is really a gallon.
> SEE: скриншот новости о проверке округа Батлер; сток — инспектор со сканером

Here's the rule they test against. Scan a hundred items, and a store is allowed two mistakes. A third one, and it fails.
> SEE: сетка 100 ценников, две красные клетки, третья — штамп FAIL

In October twenty twenty-two, they walked into every Dollar General in the county. All twenty stores.
> SEE: карта округа, 20 точек

Every single one failed. [PAUSE]
> SEE: 20 точек по очереди краснеют

The best store was wrong on almost seventeen percent of the items they checked. The worst one, on Eaton Road in Hamilton, had seventeen items checked. Fifteen rang up different from the shelf.
> SEE: скриншот Springfield News-Sun «17 items were checked and 15 had different prices»; 17 иконок, 15 красных

Eighty-eight percent. In that store, a price tag was right about one time in eight.
> SEE: крупно «88%»

A six pack of Diet Coke, four dollars on the shelf, five twenty-five at checkout. A Snapple, one dollar on the shelf and a dollar fifty at the register. In a store with the word dollar in its name.
> SEE: три пары «полка → касса» по очереди; на последней — вывеска Dollar General, слово «Dollar» подсвечено

To be fair, one store in the county made its mistakes in the customer's favor. One.
> SEE: одна зелёная точка среди красных

Ohio's attorney general sued. In his words, this seems like a company trying to make an extra buck and hoping no one will notice. Dollar General settled for one million dollars and agreed to fix its prices.
> SEE: скриншот пресс-релиза генпрокурора Огайо; цитата Йоста дословно («This seems like…») с источником

So how does a price tag end up this wrong in the first place?

## PART 3

When Dollar General changes a price, the new number goes straight into the registers. Automatically.
> SEE: схема: штаб-квартира → линии в кассы, вспыхивают мгновенно

The shelf tag doesn't change by itself. Somebody in the store has to print the new label, find the right shelf, and swap it by hand. According to the federal lawsuit, store policy has workers changing those stickers every week.
> SEE: сток — работник с ценниками у полки; маркер на строке FindLaw про еженедельную замену

And in some stores, at some hours, there are only one or two employees on duty. If one of them is at the register, who's swapping the stickers?
> SEE: пустой зал магазина; одна касса, один человек

The register always knows the new price. The shelf only knows the old one. That's where the gap opens. [PAUSE]
> SEE: сплит: слева ценник «LAST WEEK», справа касса «TODAY»

The tag is a photo of last week. The register is today's bill. And when prices keep going up, an old tag usually shows a smaller number than the register does. You find out at checkout, if you find out at all.
> SEE: стрелка цены ползёт вверх; ценник отстаёт

Dollar General says occasional price discrepancies can happen despite its policies. It says its goal is one hundred percent accuracy between shelf and register, and that store teams are expected to make things right for the customer.
> SEE: заявление компании на экране с источником (Fortune/Fox4)

But in a court case, the company also argued that matching them one hundred percent of the time is virtually impossible.
> SEE: скриншот Axios с маркером на «virtually impossible»

Nobody asked for a hundred. The standard is ninety-eight.
> SEE: «100%» зачёркнуто, рядом «98%»

And if this were only about stale stickers, a one million dollar settlement should have fixed it. So we checked what happened after Ohio.
> SEE: календарь листается 2022 → 2023 → 2024

## PART 4

Before Ohio, Vermont had already settled with Dollar General in twenty nineteen, for one point seven five million dollars.
> SEE: карта, Вермонт загорается с суммой

After Ohio came Wisconsin, eight hundred fifty thousand dollars. Then New Jersey. Two years after the Wisconsin deal, according to the Guardian, Dollar General stores in Wisconsin were still failing about thirty-one percent of their price checks.
> SEE: штаты по очереди; скриншот Fortune с маркером на «31%»

When Wisconsin's inspectors did catch an overcharge, it averaged seventeen percent above the shelf price.
> SEE: ценник $10 → касса $11.70 (пример расчёта, подпись «example»)

Iowa. Eight inspections at seven stores. Eight failures.
> SEE: восемь штампов FAIL

Pennsylvania. Around nine hundred stores, and more than forty percent of their inspections failed. In one store, seventy-two percent of the items were priced wrong.
> SEE: скриншот статьи Guardian/Yahoo о Пенсильвании

Then the Guardian pulled inspection records from forty-five states. Since twenty twenty-two, Dollar General stores had failed more than four thousand three hundred government price checks, in twenty-three states.
> SEE: скриншот Guardian (перепечатка); карта — 23 штата, счётчик до 4,300

And inspectors only see a sample. They don't stand behind you at the register. You're the only inspector most of your receipts will ever get.
> SEE: покупатель смотрит в длинный чек

## PART 5

So who's actually paying for all this? Start with who shops there.
> SEE: сток — маленький городок, Dollar General у дороги

According to the federal lawsuit, about seventy-five percent of Americans live within five miles of a Dollar General. About eighty percent of its stores are in towns of twenty thousand people or fewer. And households making thirty-five thousand dollars a year or less bring in sixty percent of its revenue.
> SEE: карта с кругами 5 миль; титры цифр по одной

Marc Dann, Ohio's former attorney general, now represents shoppers in these cases. He describes them as people with ten, twenty, maybe twenty-five dollars to spend that week, adding it up in their heads as they shop.
> SEE: сток — рука держит три купюры; корзина

The worst part, he said, is not that they're getting overcharged. It's that they're putting stuff back. [PAUSE]
> SEE: товар медленно возвращается на полку

Now the other side of the math. We found eight pricing settlements Dollar General has signed with seven states. Add them all up, counting New York's donation, and you get about eight point six million dollars.
> SEE: восемь сумм складываются в столбик → $8.6M

Last fiscal year, Dollar General sold forty-two point seven billion dollars worth of stuff. That's almost five million dollars every hour.
> SEE: часы; счётчик выручки крутится

So all those state settlements together cost Dollar General less than two hours of sales. [PAUSE]
> SEE: часы останавливаются на 1:46; титр «8 state settlements = under 2 hours of sales (revenue)» и «our calculation»

And the shoppers who paid four thirty-five for a two dollar creamer? In Ohio, most of that million went to food banks. In New York, this October, the deal was a donation of food and household goods. Good causes. But the New York deal includes no refunds for the shoppers who overpaid.
> SEE: скриншоты Journal-News и Supermarket News; чек с пустой строкой «REFUND»

Shoppers could sue together, and one class action did settle. To get cash, you needed proof. Photos or receipts, or a complaint filed within thirty days. Be honest. Have you ever photographed the price tag on a creamer? The claim window closed in April.
> SEE: форма заявки; поле «photo of shelf price» пустое; штамп CLOSED

And if you shop through the Dollar General app or website, the terms say disputes go to private arbitration. One customer at a time. Thirty members of Congress wrote to the company's CEO about it.
> SEE: мелкий шрифт условий, маркер на «final and binding arbitration»; скриншот статьи о письме Конгресса

## PART 6

Which brings us back to this October, and those undercover shoppers. In early twenty twenty-six, federal investigators went undercover in Dollar General stores in four states. According to the lawsuit, some of their items rang up cheaper than advertised. But every single total came out higher. [PAUSE]
> SEE: карта, 4 штата; чеки тайных покупателей — итог красным

One California shopper added up her order using Dollar General's app and online deals. Thirty-seven sixty-one. She was charged fifty seventy-two.
> SEE: телефон с корзиной $37.61 → чек $50.72 (реконструкция)

Dollar General denies the allegations and says it will defend itself vigorously. A judge hasn't ruled. But until one does, here's how to make the price tag the price.
> SEE: здание суда; переход в магазин

Watch the screen while your items scan, not the bag.
> SEE: экран кассы крупно

Before a sale item goes in your basket, take a photo of the tag. Two seconds. That's the kind of proof that class action asked for.
> SEE: телефон фотографирует ценник

Check the receipt before you leave the store. At home it's a story. At the counter it's a refund.
> SEE: чек у кассы, палец ведёт по строкам

And know your state. In Connecticut, if an item rings up higher than the shelf, you get one free, up to twenty dollars. You just have to ask. In Michigan, you're owed the difference, and the store can add a bonus of up to five dollars. In New York, Colorado and Pennsylvania, Dollar General has agreed to charge the lowest price posted.
> SEE: карта, три карточки штатов с правилом и источником

If it keeps happening, report it to your state's weights and measures office, or to reportfraud dot ftc dot gov.
> SEE: адрес на экране

Remember, those twenty failed stores in Ohio started with one letter from one shopper.
