Wichita, Kansas. Nineteen twenty-one. A cook named Walter Anderson and a real estate man named Billy Ingram open a tiny restaurant that looks like a castle. A white castle.
They have a problem nobody today would think about. Americans are scared of hamburger. Back in 1906, a novel called The Jungle exposed what was going on inside the meatpacking plants. And ground beef got a reputation as the stuff you made out of whatever was left on the floor.
So White Castle doesn't just sell hamburgers. It sells trust. The building is white because white looks clean. The beef is ground right in front of you. And Billy Ingram even paid for a study where one person ate nothing but White Castle burgers and water for thirteen weeks, just to prove it was safe.
The price? Five cents. Buy them by the sack. This was food for factory workers and night shifts and people who counted their change.
Cheap food was never charity. It was a promise. You can trust this, and you can afford this. And that promise was about to get very, very big.
