For the next few decades, the whole industry played by those rules. And whenever things got slow, everybody reached for the same lever. Price.
In 1989, Wendy's put a whole row of items on its menu for ninety-nine cents each. The value menu was born, and everybody copied it.
And in 1997, McDonald's tried the boldest price cut in its history. They called it Campaign Fifty-Five, after 1955, the year the company was founded. The idea was simple. A Big Mac, which normally cost about a dollar ninety, for fifty-five cents.
Except it wasn't simple. To get the fifty-five cent Big Mac, you had to buy fries and a drink too. And the deal changed from sandwich to sandwich. People walked up to the counter and didn't understand what they were supposed to order. Some of the restaurant owners said it was actually costing them money.
Burger King was already selling Whoppers for ninety-nine cents in some places. And about six weeks in, McDonald's announced it was killing the promotion for lunch and dinner. It was supposed to last all year. [pause]
Cheap only works when it's simple. A price you have to decode isn't a deal. It's homework. People don't want a puzzle. They want a number they can trust. Keep that in mind, because twenty-nine years later, McDonald's would learn this lesson all over again.
