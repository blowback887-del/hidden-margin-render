# T07 — The Sign Said $3.99. Your Bank Said $175. — сценарий (версия 2, после фактчекера)
Окраска: детектив «идём за деньгами» (загадка: заправка зарабатывает 10–15 центов с галлона, так кто же зарабатывает на тебе?) + сухая сатира как приправа.
Сквозная линия: одна заправка — 12 галлонов; каждая глава — новый карман, куда уходят деньги. Предмет-талисман: банковское уведомление «Pending −$175.00», возвращается в финале.
Строки `> SEE:` — что видим (не озвучиваются).

## PART 1

You put forty-eight dollars of gas in your car. Your bank app says a hundred and seventy-five. [PAUSE]
> SEE: табло колонки «$47.88»; резкий переход — телефон, уведомление «Pending −$175.00»

Nobody stole your card. That's a hold. When you pay at the pump, the station doesn't know how much you'll pump, so it blocks the biggest fill-up you could possibly buy. In 2022, Visa and Mastercard raised that limit to a hundred and seventy-five dollars.
> SEE: карта входит в слот колонки; на экране телефона замок на $175; плашка «Visa & Mastercard · $175 · 2022»

On a credit card, that's annoying. On a debit card, that's your rent money, frozen. If the account is short, the hold alone can trigger an overdraft fee. Usually it clears in a couple of hours. Some banks keep it for days.
> SEE: баланс дебетовой карты $160 → «Insufficient funds»; часы крутятся; подпись «up to 3 days»

So you'd think the station that just froze your money is getting rich off that gas. Almost half of drivers guess it makes at least two dollars a gallon. Here's the strange part. The convenience store industry's own trade group says the average station makes about ten to fifteen cents on a gallon. [PAUSE]
> SEE: скриншот статьи NACS «Who Makes Money Selling Gas?»; крупно «10–15¢ per gallon»

Twelve gallons. Maybe a dollar fifty for the station. And yet somebody's making serious money off this stop. So let's follow it, cent by cent.
> SEE: 12 галлонов на табло; монетки 10–15¢ падают в маленькую копилку; вопрос «WHO'S MAKING MONEY?»

## PART 2

Start with the sign out on the road. Three ninety-nine. Look closer. In small letters, under it, one word. Cash.
> SEE: вывеска «3.99 9/10»; камера наезжает, внизу мелкое «CASH»

The card price is often five to twenty cents more a gallon. Most people pay with a card. In Maryland, a state senator called that sign bait and switch. Since October 2024, Maryland stations have to show the card price on the sign, in numbers the same size.
> SEE: две вывески рядом: «CASH 3.99» крупно и «CREDIT 4.09» мелко; скриншот CBS Baltimore; плашка «Maryland · Oct 2024 · same size»

Other states don't bother. In Florida, a TV station found a Chevron advertising three fifteen on the road and charging a full dollar more per gallon on a card. Legal. [PAUSE]
> SEE: скриншот WPTV; «$3.15 → +$1.00 card»; штамп «LEGAL»

A week after the reporter started asking questions, the gap came down. To ninety cents. Months later, it was back to a dollar.
> SEE: «+$1.00» зачёркивается, рядом «+$0.90»; потом снова «+$1.00»

Why charge extra for a card at all? Because every swipe costs the station money. The trade group says bank fees eat six to ten cents of every gallon. Last year, convenience stores paid a record twenty-one point three billion dollars in card fees. For many of them, the trade group says, that's more than their pre-tax profit.
> SEE: карта, от неё стрелка к значку банка «6–10¢ / gallon»; счётчик «$21.3 billion · 2025»

Then there's that little nine at the end. Nine tenths of a cent. It started in the 1930s, when gas cost pennies and a full cent was a big jump. It never went away. The trade group itself says that fraction can be as much as a tenth of what a station makes on fuel.
> SEE: «9/10» крупно; архивное фото заправки 1930-х; дробь превращается в монетку размером 10% от копилки

So if the gas barely pays, what does? Here's the whole business in one line. The gallon is the bait. The store is the business.
> SEE: колонка на крючке; за ней светится дверь магазина; плашка «THE GALLON IS THE BAIT»

Fuel brings in sixty-five percent of a convenience store's sales, but less than forty percent of its gross profit. The trade group has spent years telling owners to pay the whole site's bills with what they make inside. And inside, the biggest profit maker is food and fountain drinks. Almost thirty-nine percent of in-store gross profit.
> SEE: две полосы: «sales 65% fuel» против «gross profit 38.8% fuel»; внутри магазина — автомат с газировкой, хот-доги на роликах; «38.9% of in-store gross profit»

The store is built for that. Industry design advice calls the drink cooler a traffic generator, and says to place it so it pulls you through the store. Go in for a beer, and you grab something else on the way back to the register. You came for gas. You leave with a hot dog.
> SEE: план магазина сверху: холодильник у дальней стены, путь от двери петляет мимо полок; в руке у героя хот-дог; плашка «"traffic generator"»

But you don't even have to go inside to overpay. There's a button on the pump for that.
> SEE: колонка крупно, три кнопки; палец зависает над правой

## PART 3

Regular. Plus. Premium. The word premium does a lot of work. It sounds like you're doing your car a favor.
> SEE: три кнопки; на «Premium» блестит золотая звезда

AAA tested it. In cars built for regular, premium did nothing. No extra horsepower, no better mileage, no cleaner exhaust. Their engineers put it simply. Premium means higher octane, not higher quality.
> SEE: скриншот пресс-релиза AAA; цитата-плашка «higher octane, not higher quality»

In one year, AAA estimated, sixteen and a half million drivers did it anyway. More than two hundred seventy million times. That's over two billion dollars spent on nothing. [PAUSE]
> SEE: счётчик «16.5 million drivers», «270 million fill-ups», «$2.1 billion»

And back then, premium only cost about fifty cents more. Here's what happened next.
> SEE: ценник премиума, разница «+50¢ · 2016»

For decades, premium was about twenty cents more than regular. By 2015, forty. By 2018, sixty. Past eighty in 2022. Now it's close to a dollar a gallon.
> SEE: лестница-график: 20¢ → 40¢ → 60¢ → 80¢ → ≈$1 (2026); источник EIA via CNN

So premium got that much more expensive to make? CNN went through the government data. Since 2007, the wholesale gap between premium and regular grew about twenty cents. The price you pay grew a lot more than that. [PAUSE]
> SEE: два графика рядом: оптовый разрыв почти плоский, розничный взлетает; источник EIA, CNN

CNN's conclusion was blunt. Premium gas has become pure profit for gas stations.
> SEE: заголовок CNN; плашка «pure profit»

Twelve gallons of premium your car doesn't need. Ten dollars and eighty cents. Every fill-up. Congratulations, you're a premium customer.
> SEE: чек заправки: «+ premium 12 gal × 0.90 = $10.80»

If you want to catch these before they catch you, subscribe. We take one of these apart every few days.
> SEE: кнопка подписки, логотип канала

But the sign, the store and that button are things you can see. The next one you can't. It's in the timing.
> SEE: табло колонки, цифры начинают меняться

## PART 4

Remember this spring? Oil jumped, and the sign out front jumped with it. In March, the national average went up a full dollar in one month.
> SEE: нарезка: вывеска переключается 3.29 → 3.69 → 4.19; новостной график нефти вверх

Then oil came back down. And the sign took its time. [PAUSE]
> SEE: нефть резко вниз; цифры на вывеске меняются по одному центу, медленно

Economists have a name for this. Rockets and feathers. Prices go up like a rocket and come down like a feather. It's one of the most studied patterns in the business. One study by a Federal Trade Commission economist found that, on average, prices at the pump go up three to four times faster than they come down.
> SEE: ракета взлетает, перо медленно падает; плашка «up 3–4× faster than down · FTC economist»

The St. Louis Fed watched it happen again during the Iran conflict this year. Gas shot up fast and came down slowly.
> SEE: скриншот статьи St. Louis Fed (август 2026)

Why? Part of it is us. When prices are rising, everyone's watching and comparing. When they're falling, nobody shops around. Stations can drift down slowly, because you stopped checking.
> SEE: люди в телефонах сравнивают цены, когда цены растут; когда падают — экраны гаснут

And so far, this is the most expensive November AAA has ever recorded.
> SEE: скриншот AAA «most expensive November on record»

So far, every trick has a person behind it. An owner who picks the sign, the fraction, the price of the third button. But in California, drivers say some of those prices weren't set by a person at all. [PAUSE]
> SEE: вывеска заправки ночью; на ней цифры обновляются сами, как по команде

## PART 5

In June, three California drivers filed a federal lawsuit against some of the biggest names at the pump. BP. Circle K. Marathon. 7-Eleven. Walmart. Albertsons. Together, more than seventeen hundred stations.
> SEE: скриншот AP / ABC7; ряд вывесок-силуэтов без логотипов; счётчик «1,700+ stations»

The lawsuit is about software. A pricing system from a company called Kalibrate. According to the complaint, it connects straight to the stations' pumps and signs, and uses data from competing stations to set prices.
> SEE: облако-сервер, от него провода к колонкам и вывескам; плашка «According to the complaint»

The drivers call it the central nervous system for a conspiracy. And the complaint says the software discourages stations from pricing below their rivals. Undercut the guy across the street, and it warns you about a downward spiral. [PAUSE]
> SEE: экран ПО-реконструкции с предупреждением «Downward spiral»; плашка «Reconstruction · based on the complaint»

Picture two stations on the same corner, both feeding their numbers into the same system. The price war you'd expect never comes.
> SEE: две заправки через дорогу, обе подключены к одному облаку; цены на вывесках меняются одновременно

According to the lawsuit, where stations use it, gas costs about six cents more on average. And up to thirty cents in some markets. And every extra cent costs California drivers about a hundred thirty-four million dollars a year.
> SEE: карта Калифорнии; «≈6¢ avg · up to 30¢»; «1¢ = $134 million a year»

Remember the ten to fifteen cents a station makes on a gallon? This lawsuit says the software pushed prices up as much as thirty. [PAUSE]
> SEE: копилка «10–15¢» рядом с башней «up to 30¢» · «alleged»

California had just passed a law that says sharing a pricing algorithm with competitors can count as price fixing. It took effect in January. These are allegations. Walmart said it was reviewing the complaint, BP didn't comment, and a court will decide.
> SEE: плашка «AB 325 · Jan 1, 2026»; плашка «Allegations — a court will decide»

## PART 6

So, back to that forty-eight dollar fill-up and the hundred and seventy-five dollars your bank froze.
> SEE: возвращается уведомление «Pending −$175.00»

So here's how to keep your money. On a debit card, pay inside for an exact amount, or use a credit card, so nothing gets frozen. Look for the card price before you pull in. Open your owner's manual. If it says regular, buy regular. And when prices are falling, shop around. That's exactly when nobody does.
> SEE: чек-лист из четырёх пунктов, каждый ставится галочкой

Same twelve gallons, no hold, no surprise. The station still gets its fifteen cents. It just doesn't get the rest.
